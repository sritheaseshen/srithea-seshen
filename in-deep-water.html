Oakhaven · Book Two
Enter the Deep

Somewhere between wanting and belonging lies the difficult art of choosing.

Rhea Galani has spent her life making herself impossible to leave behind.

Brilliant, restless, and accustomed to reading what other people miss, she has built a life in which competence is easier than vulnerability and independence is safer than needing anyone.

Hrishikesh has spent his life making certain that nothing he loves ever has the chance to leave at all.

Calm, strategic, and almost unnervingly observant, he has turned protection into instinct. He knows how to anticipate a problem before it arrives. He is less certain what to do when the problem is another person choosing freely.

Between them, trust becomes less a promise than a practice. A series of ordinary choices. A hand offered without expectation. A door left open. A calendar rearranged. A question asked instead of an answer assumed.

Set between Chicago and Greece, the second book of the Oakhaven Duology asks what happens when two capable people discover that love is not another system to master.

And somewhere along the way, they discover that commitment does not have to mean surrendering the self.

The People
The Deep

Hrishikesh

31+ · VP of Corporate Strategy · Rudra-Vanshaj Conglomerate

He can anticipate almost everything— except the moment protection becomes possession.

Hrishikesh is a strategist in the truest sense of the word. He reads rooms, people, risks, and silences with an ease that makes him difficult to surprise. Responsibility has made him dependable; love has made him dangerous to himself.

The Current

Rhea

28+ · Founder & Business Analyst · The Galene Corporation

She learned to survive by becoming indispensable, then discovered that being needed is not the same as being known.

Rhea is analytical, bright, and relentlessly self-possessed. She built The Galene Corporation because she trusts her own judgment more easily than she trusts anyone else's promises. Beneath that competence is a woman who wants something much harder than independence: the freedom to choose someone without disappearing inside the choice.

The Places

Two cities. One difficult tide.

Chicago

The City

Glass, winter light, boardrooms, old loyalties, and a life built at speed. Chicago is where Rhea knows exactly who she is— until Hrishikesh asks a question she cannot audit her way around.

Greece

The Sea

Salt, white stone, blue horizons, and the dangerous luxury of stillness. Greece gives them enough distance from ordinary life to discover what remains when there is nowhere left to hide.

What the Water Keeps

This is a love story about people who do not need to be rescued. It is about what happens when two whole, capable adults decide that choosing one another does not have to cost them the selves they fought to become.

Behind the Story

Two people. Their reality. Everything in between.

In Deep Water began with two people I know intimately—not as characters, but as human beings with a reality that existed long before I gave it language.

The novel is not their story reproduced on the page. It is what fiction does to reality: it takes the emotional truth, moves it somewhere unfamiliar, gives it different circumstances, and asks what else might be revealed there.

The same person who first inspired Tryambak is behind Hrishikesh too. But Hrishikesh is not entirely him. There is a shadow in him— a distortion, an exaggeration, a fictional possibility that exists only because the page allows it to.

What interested me most was not the fantasy of perfect love. It was the reality of two people learning, sometimes imperfectly, that understanding another person does not mean assuming you already know what they need.

Tide Notes
Hrishikesh is not a man who needs to learn how to protect someone. He needs to learn when protection stops being his decision to make.
Rhea does not need someone to save her. She needs someone who can remain when she chooses herself.
The deepest intimacy in this book is not being understood. It is being asked.
